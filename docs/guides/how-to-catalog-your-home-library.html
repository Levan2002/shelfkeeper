How to catalog your home library

Cataloging a home library sounds like a project for a rainy weekend, and with 500 books and a spreadsheet it is. But it doesn't have to be. Whether you want to stop buying duplicates, find a book in ten seconds, or keep track of what you've lent out, here's how to build a catalog of the books you own, and how to make it quick.

Decide what the catalog is for

Before you start, decide what you want out of it, because that determines what you record.

  • "Do I already own this?" You only need the title and author, and it must be searchable on your phone, in the bookshop.
  • "Where is it?" Add a location: the room, the shelf, the box in the attic.
  • "Who has it?" You need a lending record with a name and a date.
  • Insurance or a collector's inventory. Add edition, publisher, year and condition.

Most people want the first two, so start there. You can always add more later.

Five ways to do it

A spreadsheet

Fully flexible and free. Columns for title, author, ISBN and location work well. The cost is typing: at a minute a book, 300 books is five hours.

A notebook or index cards

Charming and robust, but you can't search it from the bookshop, and rearranging shelves means rewriting.

Scan the barcodes

Almost every book printed in recent decades has an ISBN barcode on the back cover. Scanning it identifies the exact edition, so it's the most accurate option. It's still one book at a time.

Photograph the shelf

Text recognition can read the titles on spines from a single photo, which is much faster for big collections. It isn't perfect: faded, foil or very small spine text is hard to read, and spines without a title need a second pass.

Import from somewhere else

If you already track books on Goodreads, StoryGraph or LibraryThing, you can export your list as a CSV file and import it, rather than starting from nothing. There's a separate guide on that: how to export your Goodreads library.

In practice, a mix works best: photograph shelves to get most of the way there, scan barcodes for the stubborn ones, and type the odd rare or self-published book by hand.

A step-by-step plan that doesn't take all weekend

  1. Work one shelf at a time. Finish a shelf before you start the next. A catalog that is 100% done for one room beats one that is 40% done for the whole house.
  2. Take the photo in good light. Stand straight in front of the shelf, fill the frame with spines and avoid glare. Five to fifteen spines per photo gives the best reading.
  3. Check the doubtful ones. Recognition can pick the wrong edition or a book with a similar title. Look at the ones flagged as uncertain and fix them straight away.
  4. Handle the leftovers. Books whose spines couldn't be read are best done by barcode. Older books may not have one, so search by title and author.
  5. Record the location as you go. It's far easier to note "Study, shelf 2" while you're standing there than to work it out later.
  6. Back it up. Export your list as a CSV file now and then, so the catalog isn't locked into one app.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Cataloging everything at once. You'll stop at 30%. Do a room a day.
  • Recording too much. Fields you never fill in make the job feel endless. Title, author and shelf are enough to start.
  • Ignoring editions. Two copies with different covers may be different editions, and may have different ISBNs. That matters if you collect, but not if you simply read.
  • Adding duplicates. If you scan a shelf twice, the same books can appear twice unless your tool checks what's already in the library.
  • Forgetting to update. A catalog is only useful if new books go in the day they arrive. Scan them at the door.

Doing it with Shelfkeeper

Shelfkeeper is an iPhone app for exactly this. It reads the spines in one photo, looks each book up in Open Library and Apple Books, and lets you review the matches before anything is added. Photos are read on your iPhone; only the text of a title or an ISBN is sent to look up book details.

  1. In the Library tab, tap the + button (Add Books) at the top right.
  2. Choose Scan a Whole Shelf, then Take Photo, or From Photos if you already have a picture of the shelf.
  3. Wait while Shelfkeeper reads the spines and looks up each book. When it finishes, you see a count (for example "12 books found") and a list under Found.
  4. Check any result marked Check: these are less certain and aren't selected by default. Touch and hold a result and choose Not This Book… to search for the right edition, or untick it.
  5. Books already in your library appear under Already in Your Library, so nothing is added twice. Anything unreadable is listed under Couldn't Identify; tap Search next to it to find the right book.
  6. Tap Add Books at the bottom to add all the selected ones.
  7. For stragglers, go back to + and use Scan Barcodes (point at the ISBN on the back cover and keep going, book after book), Search by Title or Author, or Enter Manually for rare, old or self-published books.
  8. Open the Shelves tab and create a shelf for each room or box, such as "Living Room" or "Attic Box 3", then choose the shelf when you add books or move a book there from its page.

The free version holds up to 50 books and 3 shelf scans, which is enough to try the whole process on a shelf or two. If it misses a few books, scan again closer to the shelf or add them by barcode. You can export the whole library as a CSV file at any time from Settings, using Export Library (CSV).
