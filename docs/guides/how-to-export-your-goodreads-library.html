How to export your Goodreads library to a CSV file

Maybe you want a backup, a copy in a spreadsheet, or a home for your books that isn't tied to one website. Whatever the reason, Goodreads lets you download your whole library as a CSV file. Here's how to do it, what's inside, and what you can do with the file afterwards.

How to export from Goodreads

The export lives on the Goodreads website. Use a browser on a computer, or ask your phone's browser for the desktop site, and sign in to your account.

  1. Open My Books.
  2. In the menu on the left, choose Import and export.
  3. Click Export Library.
  4. Wait. Goodreads builds the file, which can take a few minutes for a large library. When it's ready, a download link for the CSV file appears near the top of the page.
  5. Click the link and save the file. It's usually called goodreads_library_export.csv.

Goodreads can change its pages, so if a menu is named slightly differently, look for "Import and export" on the My Books page. If something doesn't match, Goodreads' own help pages are the authority.

What's in the file

A CSV (comma-separated values) file is plain text, one book per line. The Goodreads export includes columns such as:

  • Title, author and additional authors
  • ISBN and ISBN13 (the book's unique number)
  • Your rating, and the average rating
  • Publisher, number of pages, and year published
  • Date read and date added
  • Bookshelves, and the "exclusive shelf" (read, currently-reading or to-read)
  • Your review, and any private notes

Some books have an empty ISBN, which is common for ebooks and some older editions. They can still be imported by title and author, but identifying the exact edition is harder.

How to open and check the file

Open the CSV in Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets. If accented characters look odd, import it as UTF-8 text. It's worth scanning the first few rows to check that the ISBNs look right: spreadsheets sometimes turn long numbers into scientific notation (like 9.78E+12) if you edit and re-save them. If you only want to import the file, there's no need to edit or re-save it; leave it as downloaded.

Things to know before you move

  • Keep the original. Treat the downloaded file as your backup and put a dated copy somewhere safe.
  • Shelves and statuses carry over only if the new app understands them. The "exclusive shelf" column (read, currently-reading, to-read) is the part most apps use. Custom shelves are listed under "Bookshelves".
  • Reviews are text. They come across as plain text in the file, so formatting and links may not survive.
  • Covers aren't in the file. A CSV doesn't contain images, so an app has to fetch covers again, usually by ISBN.
  • Other services use similar files. The StoryGraph and LibraryThing can export your library too. Check their export pages for the details, and note that only files in the same layout as the Goodreads export will work with a Goodreads importer.

Doing it with Shelfkeeper

Shelfkeeper can import the Goodreads CSV directly and can write one back out again, so your library never gets stuck in one place.

  1. Save the Goodreads CSV to your iPhone, for example in the Files app or iCloud Drive.
  2. In Shelfkeeper, tap the Settings button (the gear) at the top of the Library tab.
  3. Under Your Data, tap Import from Goodreads and choose the file. If it doesn't look like a Goodreads export (it needs a "Title" column), Shelfkeeper tells you.
  4. When the import finishes, you see a summary such as how many books were added and how many were already in your library. Covers download in the background.

Here is how the data is mapped. Books marked "to-read" go to your Wishlist (unless the file says you own a copy). "Read" and "currently-reading" become the matching reading status, with your rating and the date you finished. Your review and private notes are combined into the book's notes. A Goodreads custom shelf becomes a Shelfkeeper shelf (a book goes on the first of its custom shelves), and the standard read, to-read, currently-reading and favorites shelves aren't turned into shelves. Books already in your library are skipped, so you can import the same file twice without making duplicates.

The free version holds up to 50 books. If your file has more, Shelfkeeper imports up to the limit and tells you how many more need Shelfkeeper Pro. To go the other way, use Export Library (CSV) in the same place: it writes a Goodreads-compatible file you can open in Numbers or Excel, or import elsewhere.
